5.2 Abstract
This project addresses a practical digital food-safety challenge: people with food allergies frequently rely on online recipes that are distributed across multiple websites with inconsistent formats, variable reliability and uneven transparency about ingredient risk. The research aim was to design and evaluate a multi-source recipe scraping system that supports allergen-aware retrieval and filtering while maintaining ethical scraping behaviour and clear user-facing diagnostics when sources are unavailable. A design-science and iterative engineering approach was used, combining source-specific scrapers, shared extraction utilities, API-driven aggregation, and a rule-based allergen detector aligned to the 14 major allergens. The implemented system was evaluated quantitatively using benchmark queries across active sources, with metrics for query success, source availability, extraction completeness, formatting quality, latency, and allergen detection/filtering effectiveness. Results showed strong operational reliability within the benchmark window, high extraction completeness, and strong safety-oriented allergen behaviour with high recall and low false-negative risk under the silver-truth protocol. Source diagnostics also improved transparency by distinguishing successful, partial, and unavailable states. The project concludes that a diagnostics-driven multi-source architecture can provide a practical and defensible foundation for allergen-aware recipe retrieval, while highlighting future requirements for lower-latency optimisation and externally labelled clinical validation of allergen detection.

5.3 Introduction

5.3.1 Motivation for the Project
The growth of online cooking platforms has made recipe discovery easier, but it has also created a fragmented information environment for users who must manage food allergies. Recipes are spread across sites with different page structures, inconsistent metadata, and varying levels of ingredient clarity. For users with allergy risk, this inconsistency is not merely inconvenient; it can reduce trust and increase cognitive load when checking ingredient safety manually. Existing recipe search experiences also tend to fail silently when sources are blocked or unavailable, which can mislead users about whether no recipes exist or whether data retrieval has failed.

From a technical perspective, multi-source scraping is challenged by heterogeneous HTML structures, changing markup, source-side anti-bot controls, and policy constraints. From a user perspective, allergen filtering is only useful if recipe extraction quality is high and system behaviour is transparent under failure conditions. These combined practical and research challenges motivated the project.

5.3.2 Research Aim and Hypothesis
The research aim of this project is to design, implement, and evaluate a multi-source recipe scraping application that provides allergen-aware recipe retrieval with reliable service behaviour and explicit source-status transparency.

The working hypothesis is that a modular, source-specific, diagnostics-driven architecture can provide:
(a) reliable recipe retrieval across multiple live sources,
(b) usable extraction quality for key recipe fields, and
(c) safety-oriented allergen detection/filtering performance suitable for assistive decision support,
even when individual sources vary in accessibility or structure.

5.3.3 Research Objectives (SMART)
To operationalise the aim, the following SMART objectives were defined:

O1 (Specific, Measurable): Implement and deploy multi-source scraping across active sources, measured by source-level success and recipe yield per query.

O2 (Specific, Measurable): Achieve high extraction completeness for core fields (title, ingredients, instructions, source, URL), measured through completeness scoring on benchmark results.

O3 (Measurable, Realistic): Integrate allergen detection and exclusion filtering aligned to the 14 major allergens, measured through precision/recall/F1 and safety filtering metrics.

O4 (Achievable, Timely): Ensure robust behaviour under partial source disruption, measured by unavailable-rate tracking, fallback activation, and user-visible status diagnostics.

O5 (Specific, Professional): Preserve ethical and transparent operation through robots-aware handling, controlled request behaviour, and explicit source-status communication.

O6 (Timely): Produce reproducible evidence of effectiveness within the project schedule through scripted benchmark pipelines and structured outputs suitable for dissertation reporting.

Following early review and implementation overlap, objectives were refined from the original proposal to better reflect safety-critical expectations. In particular, objective emphasis shifted from a nominal accuracy threshold to safety-oriented performance (minimising false negatives), confidence-aware output transparency, and explicit source diagnostics under partial failure. These refinements were necessary to align the project with ethical and professional requirements for risk-sensitive digital tools.

5.3.4 Planned Research Approach
The project follows a design-science style approach with iterative build-test-refine cycles. The system is implemented as layered components: a frontend presentation layer, a Flask API layer, source-specific scraper modules with shared request/parsing utilities, and a post-processing layer for allergen detection and filtering. This architecture was selected to separate source-specific logic from reusable resilience and quality controls.

Evaluation uses mixed quantitative and practical criteria. Quantitative benchmarking captures retrieval success, source availability, completeness, formatting quality, latency, and allergen metrics. Practical evaluation considers user-facing transparency, graceful degradation, and professional compliance behaviour. Statistical summaries and confidence intervals are used to support defensible interpretation rather than relying only on anecdotal testing.

5.3.7 Project Scope and Delimitations
This project is scoped as an applied software engineering and evaluation study, rather than a clinical medical decision-support trial. The core scope includes automated recipe retrieval from multiple public websites, data normalisation for key recipe fields, allergen inference from ingredient text, exclusion filtering based on user-selected allergens, and explicit source-status transparency in the user interface and API. The project does not claim to replace specialist dietary advice, nor does it claim regulatory certification for medical safety.

Several deliberate delimitations were applied. First, extraction was designed around websites that can be accessed ethically within a robots-aware and rate-limited process. Sources that are technically reachable but operationally unstable or structurally unsuitable were not prioritised for the final active source list. Second, allergen inference is ingredient-text driven and therefore limited by recipe authoring quality, synonym variation, and omissions in source text. Third, benchmark testing focuses on representative query sets and repeated operational runs but does not attempt to exhaustively model all global cuisines, all ingredient taxonomies, or long-run temporal drift across months of website updates.

These delimitations are appropriate to the dissertation timeframe and to the stated objective of evaluating a practical, transparent, multi-source tool under realistic constraints. They also support defensible interpretation: the project makes bounded claims about implemented system effectiveness, rather than over-extending conclusions into domains not directly measured.

5.3.8 Assumptions and Risk Management at Project Start
At project initiation, five assumptions were made. (1) Public recipe sites would expose at least minimal machine-readable structure for a subset of pages. (2) A modular scraper architecture would reduce maintenance cost when page structures changed. (3) Allergen detection based on lexical signals in ingredients would produce useful assistive output, even if not clinically perfect. (4) Source failures would be common enough to require explicit diagnostics and graceful degradation. (5) Evaluation quality would depend on reproducible scripted metrics rather than ad hoc examples.

These assumptions informed early risk controls. A shared base scraper was used to centralise request policy and diagnostics. Source-specific parsers were isolated to constrain regression impact when one source changed structure. Structured output schemas were enforced to support completeness measurement and downstream filtering. Confidence-aware allergen evidence fields were included to make uncertainty visible rather than hidden. Finally, benchmark scripts were introduced early so that architectural decisions could be tested against evidence, not only developer perception.

The practical implication of this risk posture is that the project prioritises transparency and repeatability over unsupported claims of perfect recall/precision or guaranteed source permanence. This alignment between assumptions, controls, and evidence underpins the methodological coherence of the remaining chapters.

These considerations shaped design, implementation, and evaluation decisions throughout the project.

5.4 Research Context

5.4.1 Public Health Motivation and Regulatory Context
The public health context for this project is shaped by two converging realities: rising allergy burden and fragmented digital food information. Kelly (2024) reports a sustained increase in allergy-related burden in England over a long observation period, including serious adverse outcomes such as anaphylaxis admissions. This indicates that allergen management is not a niche dietary preference issue, but an ongoing safety challenge with clear clinical and social consequences.

At the same time, food decision-making has shifted strongly into digital spaces, including recipe websites and social cooking platforms. Regulatory controls are strongest for packaged food and formal food service channels, but online recipe ecosystems are comparatively heterogeneous and less uniformly governed in practice. The resulting gap is important for this dissertation: users are making high-stakes ingredient decisions in environments where data quality, update frequency, and presentation standards vary substantially by source.

The post-Natasha's Law policy landscape has improved allergen communication in some contexts but does not automatically resolve the practical risks users face when combining multiple online recipe sources. This supports the need for assistive systems that can aggregate, normalise, and evaluate recipe ingredients while communicating uncertainty clearly. Critically, such systems must be designed so that convenience does not override safety transparency.

Anaphylaxis UK (2023) provides clinically grounded framing through the 14 major allergens and practical management considerations, including hidden sources and cross-context variation in ingredient expression. This highlights a key engineering implication: useful allergen support requires more than simple keyword filtering and must handle varied ingredient phrasing, compound ingredients, and uncertain cases. It also reinforces a user-experience implication: systems should expose confidence and evidential basis rather than presenting binary certainty.

5.4.2 Technical Literature on Web Scraping and Data Collection
Khder (2021) and Sharma and Borkar (2024) together provide a strong baseline for understanding scraping techniques across static and dynamic content contexts. Khder (2021) discusses strategy categories such as targeted extraction and broader crawling, while Sharma and Borkar (2024) examine dynamic rendering challenges and associated extraction approaches. Taken together, these works show technical feasibility but also reveal substantial variability in reliability depending on page architecture and runtime behaviour.

For this project, the practical takeaway is that recipe extraction cannot be treated as a single uniform parsing task. A robust system requires source-specific discovery and extraction pathways, with shared controls for request behaviour, retries, and diagnostics. This design choice aligns with the dissertation's implemented architecture, where adapters encapsulate source logic while common layers handle resilience and standardisation.

Suwalka et al. (2023) and Chaudhari et al. (2020) are particularly relevant because they move from generic scraping discussion to recipe-oriented pipelines. Their multi-stage framing (collection, cleaning, analysis) is directly compatible with this dissertation's technical flow: source retrieval, schema normalisation, quality checks, and allergen-aware post-processing. These studies support the argument that recipe systems must explicitly handle data heterogeneity rather than assuming stable, uniform source structures.

However, the literature also reveals a common limitation: many technical studies prioritise extraction throughput or recommendation novelty while under-reporting user-facing failure transparency. For an allergy-sensitive application, this omission is consequential. A technically successful parser that fails silently under source disruption is unsafe in practical terms, even if aggregate extraction metrics appear strong.

5.4.3 Automated Allergen Detection Literature
Roither, Kurz, and Sonnleitner (2022) and Sharma et al. (2025) demonstrate strong progress in algorithmic allergen identification and recommendation-oriented filtering. Their contributions show that multi-layer detection strategies (lexical cues, contextual signals, structured reasoning) can produce high practical performance. These studies informed the design decision to implement layered detection semantics with confidence outputs, rather than single-pass keyword flags.

Yet these works also foreground an unresolved issue central to this project: performance reporting in health-adjacent contexts often emphasises conventional accuracy metrics without sufficient discussion of asymmetric error harm. For allergen-sensitive users, false negatives carry disproportionate risk relative to false positives. This means model evaluation must be safety-oriented, with explicit false-negative reporting and conservative interpretation. The dissertation's filtering strategy and metric set are designed around this principle.

Trzmielewski (2024) extends the discussion through ontology and contextual utility framing, arguing that allergen reasoning quality depends on structured domain knowledge and context-aware interpretation. This perspective is valuable for future extensions of this project, especially for improving precision in ambiguous categories. While the current implementation remains primarily rule-driven for practicality and transparency, ontology-informed disambiguation remains a clear future direction supported by the literature.

5.4.4 Ethical, Legal, and Professional Frameworks
Brown et al. (2024) provide a broad ethical framework for web scraping, including respect for source systems, data stewardship, and research integrity. The Food Standards Agency (2022) policy context further emphasises operational expectations such as responsible request behaviour and attribution principles. These sources informed this dissertation's governance stance: robots-aware access, controlled pacing, transparent source attribution, and explicit status communication under degraded conditions.

A critical insight from the literature is that ethical scraping is necessary but not sufficient for health-adjacent tools. Even ethically collected data can create user harm if uncertainty is obscured or if system limitations are not made visible. This is why the dissertation treats diagnostics and confidence communication as core product requirements rather than optional interface features.

Commercial scraping discussions (for example, Food Data Scraper (no date)) also underline sustainability concerns for ongoing data collection, including source relationship stability and update discipline. This supports the dissertation's emphasis on monitoring and governance recommendations, rather than assuming source permanence after initial integration.

5.4.5 Critical Synthesis and Identified Research Gap
Across the reviewed literature, three gaps are most relevant to this dissertation.

Gap 1: Safety framing is often weaker than technical framing.
Many studies demonstrate algorithmic capability, but fewer explicitly centre the ethical implications of error asymmetry for allergy-sensitive users. This dissertation addresses that by prioritising safety-oriented metrics and conservative interpretation.

Gap 2: Scraping ethics and downstream user safety are frequently treated separately.
Existing work discusses responsible scraping and allergen modelling but rarely integrates them into a single evaluative framework. This dissertation contributes an integrated approach that measures reliability, extraction quality, safety filtering, and transparency together.

Gap 3: Operational transparency is under-emphasised.
Literature often focuses on extraction success while under-reporting how failure states are communicated to end users. For practical safety, this is a major omission. The implemented source-status model and API/UI diagnostics directly address this gap.

The resulting research position is not that automated allergen support can provide absolute certainty; rather, it is that a responsibly designed assistive system can provide practical value when limitations are explicit, measurable, and continuously monitored.

5.5 Practical Research Work
5.5.1 Requirements Definition
The project aimed to design and evaluate a multi-source recipe discovery web application that aggregates recipe content from several public websites, normalises the extracted data, and provides user-facing filtering and transparency features. Functional requirements were defined as follows: (1) accept a natural-language food query and return relevant recipes; (2) support multiple active sources (BBC Food, BBC Good Food, Tom Kerridge, Food - a Fact of Life, The Real Food Dietitians, and Ocado); (3) extract and display key fields (title, description, ingredients, instructions, image, metadata); (4) support allergen detection and exclusion filtering; and (5) provide robust behaviour under partial source failure (e.g., temporary source unavailability).

Non-functional requirements included: respectful crawling behaviour, practical response times, graceful degradation when sources are unavailable, and clear communication of data provenance and extraction confidence. A key constraint identified during implementation was source-specific anti-bot behaviour (HTTP 403), which introduced variability in data coverage and required explicit resilience strategies.

5.5.2 Product Design
The system was designed as a layered architecture:

Presentation layer: HTML/CSS/JavaScript client handling user input, result rendering, and source/status messaging.
Application/API layer: Flask endpoints (/api/search, /api/recipe, /api/featured) responsible for request handling, source selection, fallback logic, and response composition.
Extraction layer: source-specific scraper classes inheriting shared behaviour from a common base scraper (request handling, robots.txt checks, retry logic, parsing utilities).
Post-processing layer: allergen detection and recipe filtering based on user exclusions.
This modular design was selected to isolate source-specific parsing rules while reusing common concerns (timeouts, retries, diagnostics, data normalization). Alternative designs considered included a single generic parser and headless-browser-first extraction. The former was rejected due to poor robustness across heterogeneous markup; the latter was rejected at this stage due to complexity and cost, and because many pages expose structured data without full browser automation.

5.5.3 Implementation
Implementation progressed iteratively:

Initial stable extraction from BBC Food.
Extension to additional active sources using source-specific search/list/sitemap discovery and detail-page scraping.
JSON-LD-first extraction strategy with HTML fallback.
Resilience enhancements for blocked sources (retry, source diagnostics, fallback pathways).
Data quality improvements (markdown cleanup to remove embedded image URLs and link artefacts from instruction text).
API-level source status reporting (ok, partial, temporarily_unavailable, no_results, etc.) for transparent UI feedback.
A practical engineering decision was to prioritise user-visible reliability over hard failure: when one or more sources fail, the API still returns successful response objects with source-level diagnostics and optional demo fallback, rather than returning a total error state.

5.5.4 Testing Approach
Testing combined functional, integration, robustness, and quality-oriented checks:

Functional API tests: endpoint behaviour, input validation, source filtering, allergen filtering.
Extraction validity checks: presence/shape of required fields per recipe.
Robustness tests: repeated runs under known 403 conditions and verification of graceful degradation.
Text quality checks: detection of markdown artefacts, raw URLs in instructions, malformed steps.
UI response checks: status banners and unavailable-source messaging.
The test strategy deliberately included both success-path and failure-path scenarios to reflect realistic operational behaviour in web scraping systems.

5.5.5 Tools and Technologies
The system was built using the following core technologies. The backend is implemented in Python using the Flask web framework, with Requests for HTTP handling and BeautifulSoup for HTML parsing and structured-data extraction. The frontend uses standard JavaScript, HTML, and CSS for user interaction and result rendering. Logging and Flask test-client utilities were used for endpoint validation during development and testing. All API responses use structured JSON schemas, which support both user-facing diagnostics and machine-readable evaluation data capture.
5.5.6 Ethical, Legal, Social, Security, and Professional Considerations
The implementation includes robots checks and controlled request pacing. However, anti-bot restrictions on some sources indicate that legal and terms-of-service interpretation is not equivalent to robots.txt permissiveness. Therefore, the project explicitly documents a compliance trade-off between retrieval resilience and strict platform restrictions. Security-wise, frontend rendering escapes dynamic content to reduce injection risk, and API inputs are validated. Socially, allergen detection is presented as assistive and not medical advice; this disclaimer is shown in the interface. Professionally, source attribution and transparent error/status messaging are used to avoid misleading users about data completeness.

5.5.8 Alternatives Considered and Reasons for Rejection
A number of alternatives were considered during implementation and rejected for explicit reasons:

Alternative A: Generic parser for all sites
Rationale for rejection: websites differed significantly in structure and metadata quality, causing unstable extraction and high maintenance overhead through conditional logic. Source-specific modules provided superior maintainability and clearer fault isolation.

Alternative B: Browser-automation-first extraction
Rationale for rejection: while potentially useful for script-heavy sites, a browser-first baseline increased complexity, runtime cost, and policy ambiguity. A requests/HTML approach with structured-data preference provided sufficient capability for active sources within project constraints.

Alternative C: Single-source production scope
Rationale for rejection: this would improve operational simplicity but would not satisfy the core multi-source research aim and would weaken the practical contribution around source diagnostics and aggregation behaviour.

Alternative D: Binary allergen flags without confidence/evidence
Rationale for rejection: binary-only outputs reduce interpretability and can create false certainty. Confidence labels and evidence fields better support user trust and professional transparency.

Alternative E: Aggressive fallback behaviours regardless of policy context
Rationale for rejection: this increases legal and ethical risk. The selected approach is compliance-aware and records source state transparently, favouring accountable behaviour over opaque retrieval maximisation.

5.5.9 Detailed Methodological Protocol and Implementation Procedure
To ensure methodological repeatability, implementation and testing followed a defined protocol rather than ad hoc coding. The procedure can be summarised as seven stages.

Stage 1: Source reconnaissance and viability screening
Candidate sources were first assessed for accessibility, robots.txt compatibility, and extractability potential. This prevented over-investment in sources that were either structurally unsuitable or policy-constrained for reliable use within project ethics. The screening criteria were deliberately practical: can recipes be discovered consistently, can key fields be extracted in a stable way, and can status be reported transparently to users when extraction is not possible?

Stage 2: Source adapter implementation
Each source was implemented as an adapter class inheriting shared request, parsing, and diagnostics behaviour from a base class. This reduced duplicated code and ensured that status instrumentation remained consistent across sources. Source adapters included explicit search/discovery logic (for example, standard search pages, list-page traversal, or sitemap-led discovery) and detail-page extraction logic (JSON-LD-first with HTML fallback where required).

Stage 3: Structured extraction and normalisation
For each recipe, the system attempted to generate a normalised schema containing title, URL, source, ingredients, instructions, and optional metadata. The normalised schema was designed to enable consistent rendering and evaluation across heterogeneous sources. This standardisation step was essential for downstream allergen analysis because ingredient lists from different sites vary significantly in style and punctuation.

Stage 4: Allergen inference and evidence attachment
Detected allergens were attached per recipe, including confidence, methods, and evidence fields. This ensured that filtering decisions were inspectable and that users could see why an allergen was flagged. The system did not silently remove recipes without traceability; instead, it retained detection metadata to preserve transparency and debuggability.

Stage 5: Failure-mode handling and diagnostics
The system captured source-level state (`ok`, `partial`, `temporarily_unavailable`, `unavailable`, `error`) and linked this to API/UI messaging. This was a critical professional requirement: users should know whether low result counts reflect genuine query scarcity or upstream source limitations. Diagnostic consistency also simplified evaluation, because reliability metrics could be computed directly from structured status outputs rather than inferred indirectly.

Stage 6: Effectiveness benchmarking
Benchmark runs were executed using a scripted evaluation pipeline to produce reproducible output artefacts. Captured data included source-trial outcomes, recipe-level quality records, allergen performance metrics, confidence calibration rows, and safe-filter effectiveness summaries. These outputs persisted in machine-readable files for traceable dissertation reporting.

Stage 7: Interpretation, reflection, and refinement
Findings were interpreted against objectives, and implementation decisions were revisited where results suggested meaningful improvements (for example, source inclusion/exclusion, diagnostics messaging, and extraction heuristics). This closed-loop process linked engineering action directly to measured evidence.

This protocol demonstrates that practical development work and research-quality evaluation were integrated, not separated. The system was engineered as an evaluable artefact from the beginning, which materially improved the quality of final evidence.

5.5.10 Expanded Ethical, Legal, and Professional Governance Discussion
The project engages with a common challenge in applied web data work: technical feasibility does not automatically imply policy acceptability. Accordingly, the implemented governance stance rests on three principles.

Principle 1: Respectful access behaviour
The scraper uses robots-aware checks and controlled pacing to reduce operational burden on source platforms. This is not a legal guarantee, but it is an ethically important baseline for responsible technical practice. Avoiding abusive request patterns is especially important in an educational project where reproducibility should not come at the cost of platform harm.

Principle 2: Transparency over opacity
Where source access is constrained or variable, the system exposes this explicitly through source-status outputs rather than masking failures behind silent fallbacks. This is professionally preferable because it preserves user agency and prevents misleading certainty. Transparent degradation is a core quality attribute for systems that influence food-safety decisions.

Principle 3: Assistive framing and bounded claims
Allergen results are presented as assistive indicators, not definitive medical advice. This boundary is essential both ethically and professionally. The tool helps users triage recipe suitability but does not replace ingredient label checks, specialist advice, or personal allergy management plans. This framing aligns with observed model behaviour: high practical utility with measurable uncertainty in some categories.

In legal terms, source terms and platform policies remain dynamic and jurisdiction-dependent. For that reason, the project treats policy compliance as an ongoing operational concern rather than a one-time checkbox. A robust production continuation would include explicit source allowlists, policy mode controls, periodic reviews of robots.txt policies and terms of service, and automated alerts when access behaviour changes materially.

5.5.11 Testing Matrix and Acceptance Criteria
To make evaluation criteria operational rather than abstract, a practical acceptance matrix was defined for the final benchmark stage.

Functional acceptance criteria:
- Search endpoint returns valid JSON for all benchmark queries.
- Source selection filters operate correctly for each active source key.
- Recipe objects include core required fields and stable source attribution.
- Allergen exclusion produces deterministic reductions when matching evidence is present.

Reliability acceptance criteria:
- No unhandled API errors during benchmark execution.
- Source-state diagnostics populated for every source-query trial.
- Query-level success remains positive under mixed source conditions.
- No silent failures where missing data is indistinguishable from unavailable sources.

Quality acceptance criteria:
- Completeness score remains high across recipe outputs.
- Formatting artefacts are absent or extremely rare in rendered ingredients/instructions.
- Duplicate URLs do not dominate aggregated responses.
- Source-level output remains parsable for downstream metrics.

Safety acceptance criteria:
- False-negative risk is minimized within silver-truth evaluation constraints.
- Unsafe return rate after exclusion filtering remains low/zero in benchmark sample.
- Confidence signals are included in allergen evidence outputs.
- User-facing behaviour remains transparent where uncertainty exists.

Performance acceptance criteria:
- End-to-end runtime remains stable across repeated query runs.
- Source-level latency is measured and attributable.
- No extreme tail-latency outliers caused by unstable fallback loops.

Using explicit acceptance criteria improved project quality in two ways. First, it prevented success from being defined solely by "did scraping run," ensuring that output quality and safety behaviour were tested directly. Second, it created a defensible bridge between implementation and evaluation chapters: the same criteria used for development decisions were used for reporting outcomes.

For dissertation clarity, these criteria were mapped directly to recorded outputs rather than assessed informally. Functional criteria were checked through endpoint and schema validation in benchmark records. Reliability criteria were checked using source-state and error-rate columns in trial outputs. Quality criteria were checked through completeness and formatting artefact fields in recipe-level outputs. Safety criteria were checked through allergen metrics and unsafe-return indicators under the silver-truth protocol. Performance criteria were checked through query-level and source-level latency distributions.

This explicit mapping reduced ambiguity in interpretation and strengthened the link between objectives, implementation decisions, and chapter 5.6 results. In effect, the acceptance matrix acted as a pre-defined evaluation contract, helping to avoid retrospective metric selection bias.

5.5.12 Implementation Constraints and Design Trade-off Summary
Three implementation constraints most strongly shaped design outcomes. The first was source heterogeneity: different websites required different discovery and extraction strategies, which justified the adapter-based architecture despite added development overhead. The second was policy variability: source accessibility could not be assumed stable, requiring status-aware design and avoidance of hidden failure paths. The third was time-bounded evaluation scope: benchmark depth had to be balanced against chapter-writing and reflection requirements.

These constraints produced explicit trade-offs. More source breadth increased potential coverage but also maintenance risk. Stronger safety filtering reduced false-negative risk but could reduce precision in ambiguous cases. Rich diagnostics improved transparency but increased implementation complexity. The final design selected reliability, explainability, and bounded claims over maximal source count or minimal latency, which is consistent with the dissertation aim and professional accountability expectations.

5.6.1 Evaluation Design and Measurement Framework

To evaluate the system as an allergen-detecting recipe scraping tool, metrics were grouped into five dimensions:

1) Retrieval and operational performance
- Query success rate
- Recipes returned per query
- Source diversity per query
- End-to-end latency (p50, p95, bootstrap CI)
- Duplicate rate

2) Source reliability and resilience
- Source success rate (with Wilson 95% CI)
- Source unavailable rate
- Anti-bot/block rate (HTTP 403/429 incidence)
- Fallback activation rate

3) Extraction quality
- Completeness score (required fields present)

5.6.5 Limitations and Validity Considerations

- Ground-truth limitation: allergen metrics are benchmarked against a conservative silver-truth baseline, not clinician-annotated labels.
- Temporal/platform dependence: website structures and anti-bot policies can change, so results require periodic revalidation.
- Latency trade-off: end-to-end query latency reflects sequential multi-source acquisition and prioritises coverage/reliability over minimal response time.
- Confidence calibration nuance: HIGH-confidence detections were highly precise in this run, while MEDIUM-confidence detections were less precise, supporting continued transparency in UI confidence messaging.

Additional validity threats include:
- Query-set representativeness: while diverse, the benchmark set does not cover all culinary domains or highly specialised diets.
- Silver-truth construct risk: lexical silver-truth can overestimate certainty where ingredient context is implicit.
- Source policy drift: future anti-bot or robots.txt changes may alter availability and therefore external validity.
- Measurement coupling: improved extraction quality can inflate downstream allergen metrics; this is beneficial operationally but complicates isolated causal attribution.

5.6.6 Outcome Against Project Aim

The updated multi-source implementation meets the project aim of providing an effective allergen-aware recipe retrieval tool: it successfully aggregates recipes from multiple live sources, preserves high extraction quality, and provides strong safety-oriented allergen detection/filtering behaviour with explicit source diagnostics.

5.6.7 Statistical Reliability and Uncertainty Discussion
Although headline rates are strong, uncertainty must still be interpreted responsibly. Wilson confidence intervals were used for proportion metrics to avoid overconfidence in small-sample normal approximations. For example, source-level success rates of 1.00 in eight trials per source still produce non-trivial lower confidence bounds, demonstrating that perfect observed rates do not imply certainty under broader deployment. This is an important methodological point for dissertation quality: reporting confidence bounds avoids inflated claims from limited runs.

Bootstrap confidence intervals were used for median latency estimates to reduce distributional assumptions. The resulting interval range supports the interpretation that the observed end-to-end latency profile is stable within the benchmark window, though still relatively high for interactive contexts. Importantly, this reflects a deliberate architecture trade-off: broad multi-source coverage and transparency were prioritised over minimal response time.

For allergen outcomes, macro-averaging was used to reduce dominance by common categories. This supports fairer interpretation of category-level behaviour and highlights where precision improvements are still needed. The observed combination of high recall with lower precision in selected categories aligns with the tool's safety-first design. Methodologically, this should be treated as a controlled trade-off rather than a defect hidden by aggregate accuracy.

5.6.8 Comparative Interpretation Against Practical Expectations
The project sought to answer a practical question: can a multi-source allergen-aware recipe tool remain useful when web sources are heterogeneous and potentially volatile? The benchmark results indicate yes, with caveats.

Compared with early unstable stages, the final system demonstrates significantly improved consistency: every benchmark query returned usable output, each active source contributed results, and no source entered an unavailable state during the run. This indicates the architecture has moved from prototype fragility to operational robustness in the tested environment.

However, practical effectiveness is not uniform across all dimensions. Latency remains materially higher than users may expect from single-source recipe search interfaces. This is not unexpected, because the system performs multiple external retrieval and parsing operations per query. In an applied context, this means future deployment should include clear loading states, progressive rendering opportunities, and caching strategies to protect user experience.

5.6.9 Contribution to Applied Computing Practice
From an applied computing perspective, the project contributes in three ways.

Second, it shows that quality and safety metrics can be integrated into a single benchmark workflow. This matters because scraping quality errors and allergen inference errors are operationally coupled; evaluating them separately can produce misleading conclusions.

Third, it provides a practical template for balancing ethical constraints with functional resilience. The system does not claim unrestricted extraction capability, but it does provide a controlled and auditable approach for sustainable operation.

5.7.1 Evaluation of Research Quality
The research quality is strongest in three areas: objective traceability, reproducible evidence generation, and explicit validity boundaries. Objectives were linked to measurable outputs and assessed against generated benchmark artefacts rather than anecdotal examples. The evaluation pipeline produced machine-readable trial, recipe, source, and allergen outputs that support auditability and repeatability. Limitations were not treated as incidental; they were reported as explicit boundaries for interpretation.

Methodological coherence was maintained by aligning metrics with the stated project purpose. Because the tool is allergen-aware and multi-source by design, the evaluation measured both retrieval and safety outcomes. This avoided a common weakness where scraping projects optimise extraction throughput without evaluating practical user risk. Confidence intervals were included where appropriate to reduce overconfidence in point estimates from limited samples.

That said, research quality remains constrained by the nature of available labels. The silver-truth approach provides reproducible benchmarking but cannot substitute for independently validated clinical annotation. Therefore, claims were appropriately bounded to assistive-system effectiveness rather than medical-grade correctness.

5.7.2 Evaluation of Project Journey and Process
The project journey was iterative and evidence-driven. Early phases focused on baseline retrieval and extraction correctness; middle phases addressed resilience and transparency under source variability; later phases expanded active source coverage and formalised effectiveness measurement. This sequencing was appropriate because each phase reduced a major risk: first data acquisition risk, then reliability risk, then evaluation risk.

Planning effectiveness improved over time. Initial implementation cycles prioritised feature delivery, but later cycles improved instrumentation and benchmark reproducibility, which strengthened the dissertation evidence base. This reflects a realistic development trajectory and demonstrates growth in research maturity: moving from building functionality to validating claims.

Time management trade-offs were visible. Broad source experimentation initially consumed effort on sites with weak long-term viability. However, this exploration informed better selection criteria and produced practical knowledge about policy and structural constraints. In project-evaluation terms, this was not wasted effort; it was exploratory risk reduction.

5.7.3 What Worked Well
- Modular scraper architecture reduced regression risk and enabled source-specific fixes without destabilising the entire system.
- API-level diagnostics improved user trust by distinguishing no-result conditions from source unavailability.
- Extraction quality controls produced consistently complete and clean recipe objects in benchmark outputs.
- Allergen filtering integrated smoothly into the response pipeline with transparent confidence and evidence semantics.
- Reproducible benchmark tooling enabled objective comparison and stronger dissertation reporting.

5.7.4 What Was Partial or Weaker
- End-to-end latency remains relatively high due to multi-source sequential processing and per-source network variance.
- Some allergen categories show lower precision due to lexical ambiguity and ingredient context limitations.
- Long-run reliability under policy drift is not fully demonstrated in this evaluation window.
- No human-subject usability study was conducted; usability inference is heuristic and behaviour-driven rather than participant-validated.

5.7.5 Ethical, Legal, Social, Security, and Professional Reflection
Ethically, the system is designed as assistive and explicitly non-clinical, which reduces misuse risk while still delivering practical value. Legally and professionally, robots-aware handling and transparent source attribution support accountable engineering practice. Socially, the project addresses a meaningful accessibility and safety problem by reducing manual burden for users managing allergen risk.

A core professional tension remains: retrieval robustness and strict compliance are not always aligned in web extraction contexts. The implemented approach takes a conservative stance by exposing source state and avoiding opaque behaviour, rather than optimising retrieval at any cost. This is a defensible professional choice for a project where user trust is as important as throughput.

Security controls are appropriate for the project scope: input validation, controlled rendering, and no unsafe client-side injection paths were introduced in the implemented flow. Future production hardening would still require continuous dependency review and stricter operational monitoring.

5.7.6 Reflection on Personal Learning and Project Management
The project delivered substantial personal learning in both technical depth and research discipline. Early work was primarily implementation-led, with focus on making scraping functions work source by source. As the project progressed, the emphasis shifted toward evidence quality, validity boundaries, and objective traceability. This transition from "feature completion" to "claim validation" was a key learning outcome and directly improved dissertation quality.

On the technical side, the most significant learning was that reliability in web extraction is less about clever parsing alone and more about system design under uncertainty. Source behaviour changes, policy variation, and markup heterogeneity mean that robust architecture, observability, and graceful degradation are as important as extraction logic itself.

On the methodological side, developing reproducible benchmark tooling highlighted the value of instrumented experimentation. Without structured outputs and repeatable scripts, it would have been difficult to defend conclusions with confidence. This reinforced a broader professional principle: in research software projects, measurement design should be built in early, not bolted on at the end.

Project management also evolved. Initial plans over-emphasised source breadth; later iterations prioritised source quality, reliability, and explainability. This reprioritisation improved risk control and made final outputs more coherent with assessment criteria. The main planning lesson is that scope should remain dynamic but evidence-governed: expansion should occur only when it preserves reliability and evaluability.

Areas for personal improvement include earlier formalisation of evaluation metrics, stronger up-front time allocation for chapter writing, and earlier integration of word-budget monitoring against mark weighting. These process improvements would reduce late-stage restructuring workload in future projects.

5.7.7 Critical Limitations and Mitigation Priorities
While results are strong, critical evaluation requires explicit focus on remaining limitations and practical mitigation pathways.

Limitation 1: Evidence horizon is short.
The benchmark represents a bounded time window and cannot guarantee persistent source reliability over long periods. Mitigation: implement scheduled longitudinal monitoring with alerts for source-status drift, completeness degradation, and policy changes.

Limitation 2: Allergen ground truth is indirect.
Silver-truth labelling is reproducible but not clinically authoritative. Mitigation: build a staged annotation strategy beginning with expert-reviewed subsets, then expanding to broader human-labelled datasets for robust external validation.

Limitation 3: Latency may constrain user experience.
Multi-source sequential processing increases response times compared with single-source search. Mitigation: adopt bounded parallelism, response caching, and progressive result streaming where appropriate.

Limitation 4: Precision varies across ambiguous categories.
Safety-first design appropriately minimises false negatives but introduces conservative false positives in some categories. Mitigation: add context-aware disambiguation rules, ontology support, and confidence-sensitive filtering UX.

Limitation 5: Query representativeness is finite.
Benchmark queries were intentionally diverse but not exhaustive. Mitigation: introduce rotating benchmark pools that include niche dietary contexts and less common ingredient vocabularies.

Limitation 6: Policy and legal interpretation remain dynamic.
Ethical operation requires ongoing governance, not static assumptions. Mitigation: formalise source policy review checkpoints and configurable compliance modes with documented operational behaviour.

Addressing these limitations does not undermine current findings; rather, it provides a practical quality-improvement pathway and demonstrates critical research maturity.

5.7.8 Overall Critical Reflection
Taken together, the project quality is strongest where engineering and research discipline were tightly integrated: explicit objectives, measurable criteria, and reproducible outputs. The weaker areas are not conceptual failures but expected boundaries of a time-limited applied dissertation: finite benchmark scope, partial external validation, and performance optimisation still in progress.

The key critical insight is that "effectiveness" in this domain is multi-dimensional. A system can have excellent scraping yield but poor safety communication, or strong allergen detection but weak source transparency. The final artefact avoids this imbalance by combining retrieval, quality, safety, and diagnostics in one evaluative frame. This integrated perspective is arguably the most valuable outcome of the project and should be retained in any future extension work.

5.7.9 Transferability to Similar Applied Systems
The evaluation approach used here is transferable beyond recipe aggregation. Any multi-source system with user-facing risk implications can benefit from the same principles: objective-to-metric traceability, explicit uncertainty communication, and separate measurement of reliability, quality, and safety behaviour. In that sense, the project contributes not only an artefact but also a reusable evaluation pattern for applied software research.

Transferability is strongest for domains where source variability is expected and where user trust depends on transparent failure handling (for example, health-adjacent informational tools, compliance-aware aggregators, and recommendation systems with safety filters). The main adaptation requirement is domain-specific ground-truth construction: as in this project, practical benchmarking may begin with silver-truth methods, but stronger claims require expert-labelled validation layers.

5.8 Conclusions and Recommendations
5.8.1 Conclusions
This dissertation set out to investigate whether a multi-source recipe scraping tool could provide effective allergen-aware retrieval while remaining reliable and transparent under real-world source variability. Based on the implemented system and benchmark evidence, the answer is yes within the stated project scope.

The final system demonstrates that:
1) Multi-source retrieval can be achieved reliably when source-specific extraction logic, diagnostics, and policy-aware request handling are treated as first-class design concerns.
2) Extraction quality and safety outcomes are tightly coupled; high completeness and low artefact rates materially strengthen downstream allergen behaviour.
3) Safety-oriented allergen filtering can achieve high practical utility when recall and transparency are prioritised, even if precision remains imperfect for ambiguous categories.
4) User-facing trust is improved when source-state uncertainty is explicitly communicated rather than hidden.

The project therefore contributes a practical architecture and evidence framework for allergen-aware recipe aggregation that is operationally useful, methodologically defensible, and professionally transparent. It does not claim clinical-grade allergen certainty or permanent source stability; instead, it provides a robust foundation for continued improvement.

Beyond the primary findings, the evaluation revealed several noteworthy additional observations. Confidence calibration analysis showed clear precision separation across HIGH, MEDIUM, and LOW tiers, confirming that confidence signals carry genuine discriminative value and are not merely nominal labels. Source-level analysis revealed meaningful heterogeneity in latency and yield profiles, with some sources exhibiting narrower catalogue overlap (Food - a Fact of Life) and others showing higher median response times (The Real Food Dietitians), information that directly informs future optimisation priorities. Finally, the tight coupling observed between extraction quality and allergen detection accuracy highlights that investment in upstream data quality produces compounding benefits for downstream safety behaviour, a principle that generalises beyond this specific application.

5.8.2 Recommendations for Future Work
R1: Introduce asynchronous or parallel source execution with bounded concurrency to reduce end-to-end latency while preserving source diagnostics.
R2: Add semantic disambiguation for allergen inference (ingredient ontology, context windows, and phrase-level intent) to improve precision in ambiguous categories such as sulphites and soya-related terms.
R3: Build a clinician-reviewed or dietitian-reviewed annotated allergen dataset for external validity testing beyond silver-truth approximations.
R4: Implement scheduled regression monitoring with alert thresholds for source availability drift, extraction completeness decline, and latency regressions.
R5: Extend usability evaluation with task-based user studies focused on confidence interpretation, filtering trust, and error-message clarity.
R6: Add policy-mode controls (strict, balanced, research) with explicit operational behaviour differences to support clearer governance in different deployment contexts.
R7: Expand quality scoring with additional dimensions such as semantic instruction coherence, duplicate semantic similarity, and cross-source consistency checks.
R8: Introduce caching and incremental refresh strategies to improve performance and reduce unnecessary external requests.

5.8.3 Final Statement
In conclusion, the implemented and evaluated system satisfies the core research aim as an effective assistive platform for allergen-aware recipe discovery across multiple sources. The strongest strengths are reliability, data quality, and transparent operational behaviour. The principal development priorities for the next iteration are lower latency, externally validated allergen ground truth, and long-run drift resilience.

5.8.4 Recommended Implementation Roadmap
To convert the dissertation artefact into a stronger production candidate, a phased roadmap is recommended.

Phase 1 (stabilisation; short term)
- Add asynchronous source execution with bounded parallelism.
- Introduce caching for repeated queries and recently viewed recipes.
- Add automated nightly benchmark runs with threshold alerts for drift.
- Formalise source governance lists with explicit inclusion criteria.

Phase 2 (quality enhancement; medium term)
- Improve allergen precision with phrase-level disambiguation and ingredient ontologies.
- Introduce confidence-aware UI affordances that better communicate uncertainty for MEDIUM and LOW matches.
- Expand recipe quality checks to include semantic instruction coherence and duplicate similarity thresholds.
- Add regression fixtures for known edge-case ingredient strings.

Phase 3 (validation and deployment governance; longer term)
- Build expert-reviewed allergen ground-truth datasets for external validation.
- Conduct task-based user studies with allergy-aware participants.
- Define operational compliance modes with clear behaviour guarantees.
- Implement continuous policy monitoring for source terms-of-service and robots.txt changes.

This roadmap reflects the core lesson of the project: practical utility and professional responsibility improve when capability growth is paired with explicit measurement and governance.

5.8.5 Recommendations for Future Research and Practice
Beyond implementation improvements, two research recommendations are particularly important.

First, future studies should compare alternative allergen inference paradigms (rule-based, hybrid symbolic-statistical, and transformer-assisted extraction) under the same benchmark and annotation protocol. This would clarify how much precision gain can be achieved without sacrificing safety-oriented recall.

Second, future work should include user-centred validation with participants who actively manage allergies. Quantitative system performance is necessary but not sufficient; decision confidence, interface trust, and interpretation of uncertainty are equally important for real-world utility.

For professional practice, organisations adopting similar systems should institutionalise transparency as a product requirement. Source availability, confidence signals, and uncertainty communication should be visible by design, not hidden in logs. This principle generalises beyond recipes and is relevant to other multi-source aggregation systems where user safety or trust is affected by data quality variation.

5.8.6 Closing Recommendation Set Aligned to Objectives
To maintain direct alignment with the original objectives, future action should follow this priority order:

Priority 1 (Objective O4/O5): preserve safety and transparency guarantees while scaling features.
Priority 2 (Objective O1/O3): harden reliability through drift monitoring and policy-aware governance.
Priority 3 (Objective O2): deepen extraction quality checks with semantic consistency scoring.
Priority 4 (Objective O6): expand validation quality through expert-labelled datasets and user studies.

This sequence ensures that future development does not sacrifice trust and safety for superficial feature breadth. In practical terms, that is the central lesson of this dissertation.
