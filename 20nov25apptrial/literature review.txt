Introduction

Web scraping and digital health applications have become increasingly intertwined, particularly when it comes to managing food allergens. This literature review examines recent research on ethical web scraping practices and how they apply to allergen-filtered recipe search systems. Drawing on peer-reviewed literature from 2020 to 2025, the review establishes both theoretical and practical foundations for developing web-based tools that help people with dietary restrictions. It explores the ethical, legal, and technical aspects of web scraping alongside the growing field of digital allergen management, ultimately building a case for developing an allergen-filtered recipe search application that collects data responsibly.

Food allergies have become much more common over recent decades, now affecting roughly 8% of children and 11% of adults in developed countries. This growing public health issue calls for innovative digital solutions to help people make safer dietary choices. At the same time, the explosion of online recipe databases creates both opportunities and challenges. While there's an enormous amount of culinary information available online, extracting and processing this data ethically and legally requires careful attention to established frameworks and emerging best practices. This review therefore tackles two connected research areas: conducting web scraping ethically for research purposes, and applying these techniques to create useful digital health tools for allergen management.

The Ethical Landscape of Web Scraping

Foundational Ethical Frameworks

As automated data collection has become more common across research fields, scholars have paid increasing attention to the ethics of web scraping. Brown et al. (2024) offer a comprehensive framework for understanding web scraping in research, arguing that ethical considerations need to cover legal, institutional, and scientific dimensions. They emphasise that web scraping sits at the crossroads of technological capability and social responsibility, forcing researchers to navigate complex territory where traditional research ethics don't always provide clear answers. The automated nature of web scraping, combined with the massive scale at which data can be collected, creates new ethical challenges that go beyond conventional human subjects research.

Building on this foundation, Krotov, Johnson, and Silva (2020) identify specific ethical concerns in web scraping. Their tutorial on web scraping legality and ethics highlights three main ethical issues: privacy violations, data misuse, and putting strain on target servers. They argue that even publicly accessible data may come with privacy expectations, and that aggregating such data automatically can reveal patterns or information that people never intended to share. This challenges the oversimplified view that public data is automatically free from ethical constraints. Instead, they suggest that context, purpose, and potential harm should guide ethical decisions in web scraping.

Rennie et al. (2020) provide a practical example of these ethical complexities in their study of a public health research project that scraped data from county jail websites for HIV surveillance. Their analysis shows that existing ethical frameworks, including those from Institutional Review Boards, only offer partial guidance for web scraping activities. They identify several ethical tensions: balancing public health benefits against individual privacy rights, the difficulty of obtaining informed consent for data that's technically public but not necessarily meant for research, and the risk of re-identifying individuals even when data has been anonymised. These findings suggest that web scraping for research needs tailored ethical frameworks that account for the unique characteristics of digital data collection.

Disciplinary Perspectives on Web Scraping Ethics

Web scraping ethics look different across various disciplines, with each field bringing its own perspective. Luscombe et al. (2024) examine web scraping in archaeological research, highlighting how legal and ethical frameworks in their field remain ambiguous. Their work shows that ethical considerations need to be adapted to fit the specific characteristics and needs of different research areas. They argue that researchers should actively help shape how web scraping is performed and regulated in their own disciplines, rather than simply accepting frameworks developed for other contexts. This is particularly relevant for allergen-filtered recipe search applications, which need to balance the technical requirements of web scraping with the heightened ethical obligations that come with health-related information.

Quarta and Monterossi (2023) bring a private law perspective to the web scraping debate, analysing the legitimacy of protective strategies that website managers use. Their examination of anti-scraping clauses in terms of use and property rights claims over website content reveals just how complex the legal landscape is for scrapers. They critically evaluate the judicial criteria used to determine the limits of lawful web scraping, noting that courts have struggled to establish consistent principles. This legal uncertainty reinforces why it's important to adopt conservative ethical approaches that respect website owners' preferences even when legal obligations aren't entirely clear. For developers of allergen management tools, this means ethical practice should go beyond minimum legal requirements, recognising the broader social contract between data collectors and content creators.

The Robots Exclusion Protocol and Compliance Challenges

One of the main mechanisms for ethical web scraping is the Robots Exclusion Protocol, usually implemented through robots.txt files. This protocol serves as a community standard where website owners communicate their preferences about automated access to their content. However, recent research has revealed significant limitations in how well this protocol actually works. Kim et al. (2025) conducted a large-scale study analysing 130 self-declared bots over 40 days, and discovered that bots selectively respect robots.txt directives based on how restrictive they are. Their findings show that stricter directives are actually less likely to be followed, and certain types of bots—particularly those associated with AI and search applications—frequently ignore these directives altogether.

This selective compliance raises important questions about whether robots.txt can be relied upon as an ethical safeguard. Kim et al. (2025) argue that the protocol's effectiveness is undermined by its voluntary nature and lack of technical enforcement. They note that while some bots consistently comply, others seem to evaluate robots.txt directives case by case, potentially weighing the value of the data against how restrictive the rules are. This behaviour suggests that ethical web scraping can't rely solely on technical protocols—it needs to be grounded in principled decision-making by those who design and deploy scraping systems.

The legal implications of robots.txt compliance add another layer of complexity. Chang and He (2025) examine the legal liabilities associated with violating robots.txt directives, exploring how such violations might be interpreted under contract law, copyright law, and tort law. Their analysis reveals significant ambiguity in the legal status of robots.txt—while it functions as a community standard, its enforceability in legal contexts remains uncertain. They propose that this ambiguity has become particularly problematic with the rise of large language models that need extensive training data, leading website owners to increasingly use robots.txt to restrict data collection. Chang and He (2025) advocate for clearer legal frameworks that balance the interests of data collectors with the rights of content creators, suggesting that the current legal uncertainty may actually discourage ethical behaviour by making compliance requirements unclear.

Institutional and Scientific Considerations

Beyond individual ethical decision-making, institutional frameworks play a crucial role in governing web scraping activities. Brown et al. (2024) emphasise that researchers need to navigate not only their personal ethical obligations but also institutional policies, legal requirements, and scientific norms. They propose that web scraping for research should undergo similar scrutiny to other forms of data collection, including review by ethics committees where appropriate. However, they acknowledge that traditional Institutional Review Board frameworks may not adequately address the unique characteristics of web scraping, such as the scale of data collection, the public nature of the data, and the technical methods used.

The scientific dimension of ethical web scraping concerns the quality, reliability, and reproducibility of scraped data. Huang and Lam (2025) introduce the PRITES framework (Plan, Retrieve, Investigate, Transform, Evaluate, Summarise) as an integrative approach to investigating and assessing web-scraped datasets. Their framework emphasises documenting the entire scraping process, assessing the quality of resulting datasets, and reporting methodologies transparently. This scientific rigour serves an ethical function by ensuring that scraped data is used responsibly and that research findings based on such data are reliable. Huang and Lam (2025) argue that poor quality control in web scraping can lead to flawed research conclusions, which is itself an ethical failure when such research informs policy or practice.

Technical Implementation of Ethical Web Scraping

Rate Limiting and Server Respect

Implementing ethical web scraping principles requires specific technical practices that show respect for the resources and preferences of data sources. One fundamental practice is rate limiting, which controls how frequently requests are sent to target servers. Krotov et al. (2020) identify server overload as a potential harm from aggressive web scraping, noting that excessive requests can disrupt services for legitimate users and impose costs on website operators. They recommend implementing delays between requests, typically ranging from 0.5 to several seconds, depending on the server's capacity and any crawl-delay directives in robots.txt files.

The technical literature suggests that effective rate limiting should incorporate randomisation to mimic human browsing behaviour and avoid detection as automated traffic. This approach serves two purposes: it reduces the likelihood of being blocked by anti-scraping measures, and it demonstrates good faith efforts to minimise server impact. Brown et al. (2024) note that the appropriate rate limit depends on various factors including server capacity, the nature of the data being collected, and any explicit guidelines from the website operator. They argue that researchers should err on the side of caution, implementing more conservative rate limits when there's uncertainty about appropriate access patterns.

User-Agent Identification and Transparency

Another technical aspect of ethical web scraping involves identifying scraping agents through User-Agent strings. Huang and Lam (2025) recommend that scrapers use transparent User-Agent identifiers that clearly indicate the purpose and nature of the data collection. This transparency serves multiple functions: it allows website operators to understand who's accessing their content and why, facilitates communication if issues arise, and demonstrates the scraper's commitment to operating openly rather than disguising automated access as human browsing.

The content of User-Agent strings has evolved as ethical standards have developed. Early scrapers often used generic browser identifiers to avoid detection, but contemporary ethical frameworks advocate for descriptive identifiers that include information about the project, its educational or research purpose, and contact information. This shift reflects a broader movement toward transparency in automated data collection, recognising that website operators have legitimate interests in understanding how their content is being used and by whom.

Error Handling and Graceful Failure

Technical implementations of ethical web scraping must also address error handling and failure scenarios. Brown et al. (2024) emphasise that scrapers should be designed to fail gracefully, avoiding cascading errors that might result in excessive requests or unintended behaviour. Proper error handling includes implementing timeouts to prevent indefinite waiting for responses, catching and logging exceptions appropriately, and having fallback mechanisms when primary data sources are unavailable. These technical practices serve ethical purposes by preventing unintended harm to target servers and ensuring that scraping activities stay within intended parameters even when unexpected situations arise.

Application to Food Allergen Management

The Public Health Context

Applying web scraping techniques to food allergen management needs to be understood within the broader context of public health and digital health interventions. Food allergies are a significant public health concern, with potentially life-threatening consequences for affected individuals. The European Union recognises 14 major allergen groups that must be declared in food products, reflecting how serious these dietary restrictions are. Digital tools that help people identify safe food options can meaningfully contribute to public health outcomes by reducing the risk of accidental allergen exposure.

The development of allergen-filtered recipe search systems addresses a practical need arising from the intersection of increasing food allergy prevalence and the digitalisation of culinary information. Traditional approaches to allergen management rely on careful label reading and consultation of printed recipe books, but these methods become cumbersome when dealing with the vast array of recipes available online. Automated systems that can aggregate recipe data and filter it based on allergen content offer significant advantages in terms of accessibility, comprehensiveness, and user convenience.

Advancements in Allergen Detection Technologies

Recent technological advancements have significantly enhanced allergen detection capabilities, providing a foundation for more sophisticated digital applications. Lezo (2024) discusses progress in molecular detection methods for allergenicity testing, highlighting the development of biosensors and nanoparticles that offer sensitive, selective, and cost-effective alternatives to traditional techniques. These advancements are particularly relevant for web-based allergen management systems, as they demonstrate the feasibility of accurate automated allergen identification—a capability that can be translated into digital recipe analysis tools.

Gittleman (2024) emphasises how critical it is to identify and manage food allergens to protect public health and ensure food safety. The author discusses various strategies for allergen identification and management, underscoring the need for accurate detection methods to prevent adverse reactions in susceptible individuals. This public health imperative provides strong justification for developing digital tools that make allergen information more accessible and actionable for consumers. Integrating advanced detection methodologies with web-based platforms represents a promising way to translate laboratory capabilities into practical consumer applications.

A systematic review by Lezo (2024) focusing on nanobiosensors for food allergen detection reveals the potential of combining biosensors with nanoparticles to create highly sensitive detection methods. The review notes that different nanomaterials show varying potential for detecting specific allergens, suggesting that tailored approaches may be necessary for different allergenic proteins. While these physical detection methods can't be directly implemented in web-based recipe search systems, they inform the development of algorithmic approaches to allergen identification by demonstrating the complexity and specificity required for accurate allergen detection. This understanding helps establish realistic expectations for automated text-based allergen detection systems and highlights the importance of conservative flagging approaches.

Data Quality and Reliability Considerations

A critical consideration in applying web scraping to allergen management is the quality and reliability of scraped data. Unlike some research applications where data quality issues might affect statistical conclusions but not pose immediate risks, allergen information directly impacts user safety. These heightened stakes require additional layers of quality assurance and user communication. Huang and Lam (2025) note that web-scraped datasets must undergo rigorous quality assessment, including verification of data completeness, accuracy, and consistency. In the context of allergen information, this might involve cross-referencing ingredient lists, validating allergen detection algorithms, and implementing clear disclaimers about the limitations of automated allergen identification.

The challenge of ensuring data quality in allergen management systems is compounded by the variability in how recipe websites present ingredient information. Some sites provide structured data that facilitates automated extraction, while others present ingredients in free-text formats that require natural language processing to interpret accurately. This variability introduces potential points of failure where allergen information might be missed or misinterpreted. Responsible implementation of allergen-filtered recipe systems must acknowledge these limitations and communicate them clearly to users, ensuring that people understand the tool's capabilities and constraints.

Ethical Obligations in Health-Related Applications

When web scraping is applied to health-related applications like allergen management, additional ethical obligations arise beyond those in general research contexts. Rennie et al. (2020) discuss how public health applications of web scraping must balance the potential benefits of data-driven interventions against risks to individuals and communities. In the case of allergen management systems, the primary benefit is improved access to safe recipe options, but risks include the possibility of incorrect allergen information leading to adverse health outcomes.

This risk profile necessitates specific ethical safeguards. First, systems should implement conservative allergen detection approaches that err on the side of caution, flagging potential allergens even when uncertainty exists. Second, clear disclaimers should inform users that automated allergen detection can't replace careful ingredient verification and consultation with healthcare providers. Third, the system should maintain transparency about its data sources and methods, allowing users to make informed decisions about how much to rely on the tool's recommendations. These safeguards reflect a harm-minimisation approach that prioritises user safety while still providing valuable functionality.

The ethical framework for health-related web scraping applications must also consider issues of accessibility and equity. Digital health tools should be designed to serve diverse populations, including those with varying levels of digital literacy and access to technology. This consideration extends to the presentation of allergen information, which must be clear, actionable, and culturally appropriate. Furthermore, developing such tools should involve consultation with affected communities, ensuring that the system addresses real needs and respects the lived experiences of individuals managing food allergies.

Legal Frameworks and Compliance

Jurisdictional Variations in Web Scraping Law

The legal landscape surrounding web scraping varies significantly across jurisdictions, creating challenges for researchers and developers trying to ensure compliance. In the United States, the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act has been invoked in cases involving unauthorised data extraction, though its application to web scraping remains contentious and subject to evolving judicial interpretation. Chang and He (2025) note that the legal status of web scraping often depends on factors like whether the scraper bypassed technical barriers, violated terms of service, or collected data for commercial purposes. This legal uncertainty requires developers to adopt conservative approaches that respect website policies even when legal obligations aren't entirely clear.

European jurisdictions present additional considerations through the General Data Protection Regulation, which imposes strict requirements on collecting and processing personal data. While recipe data typically doesn't include personal information, scrapers must be designed to avoid inadvertently collecting user-generated content that might contain personal data, such as comments or reviews. Brown et al. (2024) emphasise that researchers must understand the regulatory environment they're operating in, noting that legal requirements may vary not only by jurisdiction but also by the type of data being collected and the purpose for which it's used.

Terms of Service and Contractual Obligations

Beyond statutory law, website terms of service create contractual obligations that govern permissible uses of website content. Krotov et al. (2020) identify terms of service violations as a primary legal risk in web scraping, noting that many websites explicitly prohibit automated data collection in their user agreements. The enforceability of such terms has been subject to legal debate, with courts reaching different conclusions about whether terms of service violations constitute actionable legal claims. Despite this uncertainty, ethical practice suggests respecting terms of service as a matter of principle, recognising website operators' legitimate interests in controlling how their content is accessed and used.

The relationship between terms of service and robots.txt directives adds another layer of complexity. Chang and He (2025) observe that some websites use both mechanisms to communicate access preferences, while others rely primarily on one or the other. In cases where these mechanisms conflict or where terms of service are ambiguous about automated access, scrapers must make judgement calls about appropriate behaviour. They suggest that when doubt exists, the more restrictive interpretation should prevail, reflecting a precautionary approach to legal and ethical compliance.

Quarta and Monterossi (2023) provide additional insight into the private law dimensions of web scraping, examining how website operators try to protect their content through various legal mechanisms. Their analysis reveals that courts have struggled to establish consistent principles for evaluating the legitimacy of web scraping, particularly when balancing property rights against principles of data accessibility. This judicial uncertainty reinforces the importance of adopting ethical approaches that go beyond minimum legal compliance, recognising that the absence of clear legal prohibition doesn't necessarily indicate ethical permission.

Synthesis and Justification for the Current Project

### Integrating Ethical Frameworks with Practical Application

The literature reviewed above establishes a clear framework for developing an allergen-filtered recipe search system that uses ethical web scraping practices. The project proposed in this dissertation integrates multiple strands of ethical guidance: it implements robots.txt compliance as recommended by Kim et al. (2025), adopts transparent identification practices as advocated by Huang and Lam (2025), employs rate limiting and server respect principles outlined by Krotov et al. (2020), and situates itself within the comprehensive ethical framework proposed by Brown et al. (2024). This integration demonstrates how theoretical ethical principles can be translated into concrete technical implementations.

The justification for this project rests on several pillars. First, there's a genuine public health need for tools that help individuals with food allergies identify safe recipe options, as evidenced by the work of Gittleman (2024) and Lezo (2024) on the importance of allergen management. Second, the vast amount of recipe data available online represents an underutilised resource that, if accessed responsibly, can serve this need effectively. Third, recent advances in web scraping ethics provide clear guidance on how to conduct such data collection in a way that respects the rights and interests of content creators while serving the needs of end users. Fourth, the educational context of this project aligns with the purposes for which ethical web scraping frameworks have been developed—namely to advance knowledge and create public benefit rather than to exploit data for commercial gain.

Addressing Limitations and Risks

A critical aspect of justifying this project involves acknowledging its limitations and the strategies used to mitigate associated risks. The primary limitation concerns the reliability of automated allergen detection, which can't achieve perfect accuracy given the variability in how recipes present ingredient information. The project addresses this limitation through multiple mechanisms: conservative allergen flagging that prioritises false positives over false negatives, clear user communication about the tool's limitations, prominent disclaimers advising users to verify allergen information independently, and documentation of the allergen detection methodology to enable informed user assessment of the tool's reliability.

Another limitation relates to the sustainability of web scraping approaches, given that website structures change over time and may break scraping implementations. The project addresses this through fallback mechanisms, including mock data that ensures basic functionality even when live scraping fails, and modular design that facilitates updates to scraping logic when website structures change. Additionally, the project's focus on educational purposes and limited scale reduces the likelihood of causing harm to data sources, as the scraping activities remain within bounds that are unlikely to impact server performance or violate reasonable access expectations.

The work of Luscombe et al. (2024) on disciplinary-specific ethical frameworks provides additional justification for the tailored approach adopted in this project. Their emphasis on active engagement with ethical standards specific to particular research contexts supports developing allergen management tools that balance technical requirements with health-related ethical obligations. This disciplinary specificity acknowledges that health applications carry heightened responsibilities compared to general research applications, requiring more stringent safeguards and more transparent communication with users.

Contribution to Knowledge and Practice

This project contributes to both theoretical understanding and practical application in several ways. Theoretically, it demonstrates how ethical frameworks developed in academic literature can be operationalised in working systems, providing a concrete example that may inform future projects at the intersection of web scraping and digital health. Practically, it creates a functional tool that addresses a real need while modelling responsible data collection practices. The project also contributes to the growing body of work on digital allergen management, an area that has received limited attention in academic literature despite its public health significance.

Furthermore, the project's emphasis on transparency and documentation serves an educational function, making visible the ethical decision-making processes that often remain implicit in technical implementations. By documenting not only what the system does but why particular ethical choices were made, the project provides a resource for others looking to develop similar applications. This transparency aligns with the scientific values emphasised by Huang and Lam (2025) and contributes to the broader goal of establishing ethical web scraping as a standard practice rather than an exceptional consideration.

The integration of advanced allergen detection concepts from Lezo (2024) and Gittleman (2024) with practical web scraping implementations demonstrates the potential for interdisciplinary approaches to digital health challenges. While the physical detection methods discussed in their work can't be directly implemented in web-based systems, the principles of specificity, sensitivity, and conservative identification inform the algorithmic approaches used in this project. This cross-pollination between laboratory science and digital application development represents a valuable contribution to the field of allergen management.

Conclusion

This literature review has examined the ethical, legal, and technical dimensions of web scraping as they relate to developing an allergen-filtered recipe search system. The review has established that contemporary scholarship provides clear guidance on ethical web scraping practices, centred on principles of transparency, respect for data sources, compliance with technical and legal standards, and careful consideration of potential harms. Applying these principles to allergen management systems introduces additional considerations related to data quality, user safety, and the heightened ethical obligations that come with health-related applications.

The synthesis of this literature provides a robust foundation for the proposed project, demonstrating how ethical frameworks can be integrated into practical implementations that serve genuine needs while respecting the rights and interests of all stakeholders. The project's approach to robots.txt compliance, rate limiting, transparent identification, and quality assurance reflects current best practices as articulated in recent peer-reviewed literature spanning multiple disciplines including computer science, public health, archaeology, and law. By situating the technical implementation within this comprehensive ethical framework, the project contributes to the ongoing development of responsible web scraping practices while addressing a meaningful public health need through creating a tool that helps individuals with food allergies make safe dietary choices.

The interdisciplinary nature of this review, drawing on sources from Krotov et al. (2020), Brown et al. (2024), Kim et al. (2025), Chang and He (2025), Rennie et al. (2020), Huang and Lam (2025), Luscombe et al. (2024), Quarta and Monterossi (2023), Lezo (2024), and Gittleman (2024), demonstrates the complexity of developing ethical web-based health applications. Each source contributes unique insights that collectively inform a comprehensive approach to allergen-filtered recipe search systems. The convergence of these diverse perspectives supports the conclusion that such systems can be developed responsibly when grounded in established ethical principles, informed by technical best practices, and motivated by genuine public health needs.
