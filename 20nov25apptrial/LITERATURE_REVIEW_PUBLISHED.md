# Literature Review: Web Scraping Ethics and Digital Food Allergen Management Systems

## Introduction

The intersection of web scraping technology and digital health applications represents a growing area of academic and practical interest, particularly in the context of food allergen management. This literature review critically examines the research landscape surrounding ethical web scraping practices and their application to allergen-filtered recipe search systems. The review synthesises findings from peer-reviewed literature published in academic journals between 2020 and 2025, establishing a theoretical and practical foundation for developing web-based tools that serve individuals with dietary restrictions. By examining the ethical, legal, and technical dimensions of web scraping alongside the emerging field of digital allergen management, this review provides a coherent argument justifying the development of an allergen-filtered recipe search application that employs responsible data collection methodologies.

The prevalence of food allergies has increased significantly in recent decades, affecting approximately 8 percent of children and 11 percent of adults in developed nations. This public health concern necessitates innovative digital solutions that can assist individuals in making safe dietary choices. Simultaneously, the proliferation of online recipe databases presents both an opportunity and a challenge: while vast amounts of culinary information exist on the internet, extracting and processing this data ethically and legally requires careful consideration of established frameworks and emerging best practices. This review therefore addresses two interconnected research domains: the ethical conduct of web scraping for research purposes, and the application of these techniques to create meaningful digital health interventions for allergen management.

## The Ethical Landscape of Web Scraping

### Foundational Ethical Frameworks

The ethical dimensions of web scraping have received increasing scholarly attention as automated data collection becomes more prevalent across research disciplines. Krotov, Johnson, and Silva (2020) provide a comprehensive framework for understanding web scraping in research contexts, proposing that ethical considerations must encompass legal, institutional, and social dimensions. Published in Communications of the Association for Information Systems, their tutorial on the legality and ethics of web scraping emphasises that web scraping exists at the intersection of technological capability and social responsibility, requiring researchers to navigate complex terrain where traditional research ethics may not provide clear guidance. The authors argue that the automated nature of web scraping, combined with the scale at which data can be collected, introduces novel ethical challenges that extend beyond conventional human subjects research paradigms.

Krotov et al. (2020) identify specific ethical concerns that arise in web scraping contexts, highlighting three primary areas of ethical consideration: privacy violations, data misuse, and resource strain on target servers. The authors contend that even publicly accessible data may carry privacy expectations, and that the aggregation of such data through automated means can reveal patterns or information that individuals did not intend to disclose. This perspective challenges the simplistic notion that public data is inherently free from ethical constraints, instead proposing that context, purpose, and potential harm must inform ethical decision-making in web scraping activities. The tutorial provides a structured approach to evaluating web scraping projects, offering specific questions that researchers should address before, during, and after data collection to ensure ethical compliance.

The work of Rennie et al. (2020), published in Public Health Ethics, provides a practical illustration of these ethical complexities through their examination of a public health research project that scraped data from county jail websites for HIV surveillance purposes. Their analysis reveals that existing ethical frameworks, including those provided by Institutional Review Boards, offer only partial guidance for web scraping activities. The authors identify several ethical tensions: the balance between public health benefits and individual privacy rights, the challenge of obtaining informed consent for data that is technically public but not necessarily intended for research use, and the potential for re-identification of individuals despite data being anonymised. These findings suggest that web scraping for research purposes requires tailored ethical frameworks that account for the unique characteristics of digital data collection.

### Disciplinary Perspectives on Web Scraping Ethics

The ethical considerations surrounding web scraping extend across multiple disciplines, each bringing unique perspectives to the discourse. Luscombe, Richardson, and Pilaar Birch (2024) examine web scraping within the context of archaeological research in their article published in Advances in Archaeological Practice. Their work demonstrates that ethical considerations must be adapted to the particular characteristics and needs of different research domains. The authors highlight the ambiguity in legal and ethical frameworks specific to their field, noting that archaeology faces unique challenges in web scraping due to the cultural sensitivity of archaeological data and the potential for looting or site destruction if location information is widely disseminated.

Luscombe et al. (2024) advocate for researchers to actively engage in shaping how web scraping is performed and regulated within their disciplines, rather than passively accepting frameworks developed for other contexts. This disciplinary specificity is particularly relevant for allergen-filtered recipe search applications, which must balance the technical requirements of web scraping with the heightened ethical obligations associated with health-related information. The authors provide a framework that includes consideration of data sensitivity, potential harms from data dissemination, and the need for community consultation when scraping data that may affect specific populations.

The archaeological perspective offered by Luscombe et al. (2024) introduces important considerations about the downstream effects of web scraping. They argue that ethical evaluation must extend beyond the immediate act of data collection to consider how scraped data might be used or misused by others who access it. This forward-looking ethical stance has particular relevance for health-related applications, where incorrect or incomplete allergen information could lead to serious health consequences. The authors emphasise that ethical web scraping requires ongoing monitoring and willingness to adjust practices as new ethical challenges emerge.

### Privacy and Public Health Considerations

Rennie et al. (2020) expand the ethical discussion by examining web scraping in the context of public health research, specifically focusing on a project that collected data from publicly accessible county jail websites to enhance HIV surveillance among incarcerated populations. Published in Public Health Ethics, their work addresses the complex ethical terrain where public health benefits must be balanced against individual privacy rights and potential harms. The authors note that while the scraped data was technically public, the aggregation and analysis of this information created new privacy risks that existing ethical frameworks struggled to address adequately.

The public health context examined by Rennie et al. (2020) reveals several ethical considerations directly applicable to allergen management systems. First, they discuss the concept of contextual integrity, arguing that data may be public in one context but not intended for use in another. Recipe data posted on cooking websites, for instance, may be intended for individual home cooks but not necessarily for aggregation into large-scale allergen databases. Second, the authors address the challenge of informed consent in web scraping contexts, noting that individuals whose data appears on public websites rarely have the opportunity to consent to secondary uses of that information. Third, they examine the potential for harm, including both direct harms to individuals and broader social harms that might result from particular uses of scraped data.

Rennie et al. (2020) propose several principles for ethical web scraping in public health contexts that have broader applicability. They argue for transparency in data collection methods, clear communication about the purposes for which data will be used, and ongoing assessment of potential harms. They also emphasise the importance of data quality and accuracy, noting that errors in scraped data can lead to flawed research conclusions or, in health contexts, direct harm to individuals who rely on that information. These principles align closely with the requirements for allergen-filtered recipe systems, where accuracy is paramount and errors could have serious health consequences.

## Technical Implementation of Ethical Web Scraping

### Rate Limiting and Server Respect

The technical implementation of ethical web scraping principles requires specific practices that demonstrate respect for the resources and preferences of data sources. Krotov et al. (2020) identify server overload as a potential harm arising from aggressive web scraping, noting that excessive requests can disrupt services for legitimate users and impose costs on website operators. The authors recommend implementing delays between requests, typically ranging from 0.5 to several seconds, depending on the server's capacity and any crawl-delay directives specified in robots.txt files. This technical practice serves both ethical and practical purposes: it reduces the likelihood of causing harm to target servers while also decreasing the probability of being blocked by anti-scraping measures.

The implementation of rate limiting reflects a broader ethical principle of proportionality in data collection. Krotov et al. (2020) argue that scrapers should collect only the data necessary for their stated purpose and should do so in a manner that minimises impact on data sources. This principle has particular relevance for allergen-filtered recipe applications, where the goal is to provide useful information to users rather than to create comprehensive archives of all available recipes. By limiting the scope and pace of data collection, developers can balance the utility of their applications against the burden imposed on recipe websites.

The technical literature reviewed by Krotov et al. (2020) suggests that effective rate limiting should incorporate randomisation to mimic human browsing behaviour. This approach serves dual purposes: it reduces the likelihood of being detected as automated traffic, and it demonstrates good faith efforts to minimise server impact. The authors note that appropriate rate limits depend on various factors including server capacity, the nature of the data being collected, and any explicit guidelines provided by the website operator. They argue that researchers should err on the side of caution, implementing more conservative rate limits when uncertainty exists about appropriate access patterns.

### Transparency and User-Agent Identification

Krotov et al. (2020) emphasise the importance of transparency in web scraping activities, including the use of clear and honest User-Agent strings that identify the scraper and its purpose. This transparency serves multiple functions: it allows website operators to understand who is accessing their content and why, facilitates communication if issues arise, and demonstrates the scraper's commitment to operating openly rather than attempting to disguise automated access as human browsing. The authors argue that transparency is a fundamental ethical principle that should guide all aspects of web scraping, from initial planning through data collection and use.

The evolution of User-Agent practices reflects broader changes in ethical standards for web scraping. Krotov et al. (2020) note that early scrapers often used generic browser identifiers to avoid detection, but contemporary ethical frameworks advocate for descriptive identifiers that include information about the project, its educational or research purpose, and contact information. This shift reflects a broader movement toward transparency in automated data collection, recognising that website operators have legitimate interests in understanding how their content is being used and by whom. For allergen-filtered recipe applications, transparent identification is particularly important given the health-related nature of the application and the need to establish trust with both data sources and end users.

### Error Handling and Quality Assurance

Krotov et al. (2020) discuss the importance of robust error handling in web scraping implementations, noting that scrapers should be designed to fail gracefully and avoid cascading errors that might result in excessive requests or unintended behaviour. Proper error handling includes implementing timeouts to prevent indefinite waiting for responses, catching and logging exceptions appropriately, and having fallback mechanisms when primary data sources are unavailable. These technical practices serve ethical purposes by preventing unintended harm to target servers and ensuring that scraping activities remain within intended parameters even when unexpected situations arise.

The quality assurance dimension of ethical web scraping extends beyond technical error handling to encompass data validation and verification. Krotov et al. (2020) emphasise that scrapers have an ethical obligation to ensure the accuracy and reliability of collected data, particularly when that data will inform research conclusions or, as in the case of allergen management systems, directly impact user safety. The authors recommend implementing multiple layers of validation, including checks for data completeness, consistency, and plausibility. For allergen-filtered recipe applications, this might involve cross-referencing ingredient lists, validating allergen detection algorithms against known test cases, and implementing user feedback mechanisms to identify and correct errors.

## Application to Food Allergen Management

### The Public Health Context

The application of web scraping techniques to food allergen management must be understood within the broader context of public health and digital health interventions. Rennie et al. (2020) discuss how digital data collection methods can serve public health goals while raising unique ethical challenges. Food allergies represent a significant public health concern, with potentially life-threatening consequences for affected individuals. The European Union recognises 14 major allergen groups that must be declared in food products, reflecting the serious nature of these dietary restrictions. Digital tools that help individuals identify safe food options can therefore contribute meaningfully to public health outcomes by reducing the risk of accidental allergen exposure.

The development of allergen-filtered recipe search systems addresses a practical need that emerges from the intersection of increasing food allergy prevalence and the digitalisation of culinary information. Traditional approaches to allergen management rely on careful label reading and consultation of printed recipe books, but these methods become cumbersome when dealing with the vast array of recipes available online. Automated systems that can aggregate recipe data and filter it based on allergen content offer significant advantages in terms of accessibility, comprehensiveness, and user convenience. However, as Rennie et al. (2020) emphasise, the public health benefits of such systems must be carefully weighed against potential risks, including the possibility of inaccurate information leading to adverse health outcomes.

### Data Quality and Reliability Considerations

A critical consideration in applying web scraping to allergen management concerns the quality and reliability of scraped data. Unlike some research applications where data quality issues might affect statistical conclusions but not pose immediate risks, allergen information directly impacts user safety. Krotov et al. (2020) emphasise that data quality is an ethical imperative in web scraping, requiring systematic approaches to validation and verification. In the context of allergen information, this might involve cross-referencing ingredient lists, validating allergen detection algorithms, and implementing clear disclaimers about the limitations of automated allergen identification.

The challenge of ensuring data quality in allergen management systems is compounded by the variability in how recipe websites present ingredient information. Some sites provide structured data that facilitates automated extraction, while others present ingredients in free-text formats that require natural language processing to interpret accurately. This variability introduces potential points of failure where allergen information might be missed or misinterpreted. Responsible implementation of allergen-filtered recipe systems must acknowledge these limitations and communicate them clearly to users, ensuring that individuals understand the tool's capabilities and constraints.

Krotov et al. (2020) argue that ethical web scraping requires ongoing quality monitoring and willingness to adjust methods when quality issues are identified. For allergen management applications, this might involve implementing user feedback mechanisms that allow individuals to report errors or inconsistencies in allergen information, conducting periodic audits of scraped data against original sources, and maintaining logs of data collection activities to facilitate troubleshooting when problems arise. The authors emphasise that quality assurance is not a one-time activity but an ongoing commitment that extends throughout the lifecycle of a web scraping project.

### Ethical Obligations in Health-Related Applications

When web scraping is applied to health-related applications such as allergen management, additional ethical obligations arise beyond those present in general research contexts. Rennie et al. (2020) discuss how public health applications of web scraping must balance the potential benefits of data-driven interventions against risks to individuals and communities. In the case of allergen management systems, the primary benefit is improved access to safe recipe options, but risks include the possibility of incorrect allergen information leading to adverse health outcomes.

This risk profile necessitates specific ethical safeguards. First, systems should implement conservative allergen detection approaches that err on the side of caution, flagging potential allergens even when uncertainty exists. Rennie et al. (2020) emphasise the principle of minimising harm, arguing that when doubt exists about the accuracy of health-related information, systems should default to the safer option. Second, clear disclaimers should inform users that automated allergen detection cannot replace careful ingredient verification and consultation with healthcare providers. Third, the system should maintain transparency about its data sources and methods, allowing users to make informed decisions about how much to rely on the tool's recommendations.

Rennie et al. (2020) also discuss the importance of considering equity and access in public health applications of digital technology. Allergen-filtered recipe systems should be designed to serve diverse populations, including those with varying levels of digital literacy and access to technology. This consideration extends to the presentation of allergen information, which must be clear, actionable, and culturally appropriate. Furthermore, the development of such tools should involve consultation with affected communities, ensuring that the system addresses real needs and respects the lived experiences of individuals managing food allergies. The authors argue that meaningful community engagement is not merely an ethical nicety but a practical necessity for developing tools that effectively serve their intended populations.

## Legal Frameworks and Compliance

### Jurisdictional Variations in Web Scraping Law

The legal landscape surrounding web scraping varies significantly across jurisdictions, creating challenges for researchers and developers seeking to ensure compliance. Krotov et al. (2020) provide a comprehensive overview of legal considerations in web scraping, noting that the legal status of web scraping often depends on factors such as whether the scraper bypassed technical barriers, violated terms of service, or collected data for commercial purposes. In the United States, the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act has been invoked in cases involving unauthorised data extraction, though its application to web scraping remains contentious and subject to evolving judicial interpretation.

The authors discuss several landmark legal cases that have shaped the understanding of web scraping legality. In hiQ Labs v. LinkedIn, the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals ruled that scraping publicly accessible data does not violate the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act, at least when no technical barriers are circumvented. However, Krotov et al. (2020) caution that this ruling does not provide blanket permission for all web scraping activities, as other legal theories including breach of contract, copyright infringement, and trespass to chattels may still apply. The legal uncertainty surrounding web scraping requires developers to adopt conservative approaches that respect website policies even when legal obligations remain unclear.

European jurisdictions present additional considerations through the General Data Protection Regulation, which imposes strict requirements on the collection and processing of personal data. While recipe data typically does not include personal information, scrapers must be designed to avoid inadvertently collecting user-generated content that might contain personal data, such as comments or reviews. Krotov et al. (2020) emphasise that researchers must understand the regulatory environment in which they operate, noting that legal requirements may vary not only by jurisdiction but also by the type of data being collected and the purpose for which it is used.

### Terms of Service and Contractual Obligations

Beyond statutory law, website terms of service create contractual obligations that govern permissible uses of website content. Krotov et al. (2020) identify terms of service violations as a primary legal risk in web scraping, noting that many websites explicitly prohibit automated data collection in their user agreements. The enforceability of such terms has been subject to legal debate, with courts reaching different conclusions about whether terms of service violations constitute actionable legal claims. The authors discuss the concept of "browsewrap" versus "clickwrap" agreements, noting that the enforceability of terms of service may depend on whether users had clear notice of the terms and explicitly agreed to them.

Despite uncertainty about the legal enforceability of terms of service, Krotov et al. (2020) argue that ethical practice suggests respecting terms of service as a matter of principle, recognising website operators' legitimate interests in controlling how their content is accessed and used. The authors note that violating terms of service, even if not legally actionable, can damage relationships with data sources and undermine the broader ecosystem of data sharing. For allergen-filtered recipe applications, this suggests that developers should carefully review the terms of service of target websites and, where possible, seek explicit permission for scraping activities or use officially provided APIs.

The relationship between terms of service and robots.txt directives adds another layer of complexity. Krotov et al. (2020) observe that some websites use both mechanisms to communicate access preferences, while others rely primarily on one or the other. In cases where these mechanisms conflict or where terms of service are ambiguous about automated access, scrapers must make judgement calls about appropriate behaviour. The authors suggest that when doubt exists, the more restrictive interpretation should prevail, reflecting a precautionary approach to legal and ethical compliance. This conservative stance is particularly appropriate for health-related applications where the consequences of legal disputes could include loss of access to important data sources.

## Synthesis and Justification for the Current Project

### Integrating Ethical Frameworks with Practical Application

The literature reviewed above establishes a clear framework for developing an allergen-filtered recipe search system that employs ethical web scraping practices. The project proposed in this dissertation integrates multiple strands of ethical guidance: it adopts transparent identification practices, employs rate limiting and server respect principles outlined by Krotov et al. (2020), and situates itself within the public health ethical framework discussed by Rennie et al. (2020). This integration demonstrates how theoretical ethical principles can be translated into concrete technical implementations.

The justification for this project rests on several pillars. First, there exists a genuine public health need for tools that assist individuals with food allergies in identifying safe recipe options. Second, the vast amount of recipe data available online represents an underutilised resource that, if accessed responsibly, can serve this need effectively. Third, the ethical frameworks developed in academic literature provide clear guidance on how to conduct such data collection in a manner that respects the rights and interests of content creators while serving the needs of end users. Fourth, the educational context of this project aligns with the purposes for which ethical web scraping frameworks have been developed, namely to advance knowledge and create public benefit rather than to exploit data for commercial gain.

Luscombe et al. (2024) emphasise the importance of disciplinary-specific ethical frameworks, arguing that researchers must adapt general ethical principles to the particular characteristics of their fields. For allergen-filtered recipe systems, this means recognising the heightened ethical obligations that accompany health-related applications. The system must not only respect the rights of recipe website operators but also prioritise the safety of end users who rely on allergen information. This dual ethical obligation requires conservative approaches to allergen detection, clear communication about system limitations, and ongoing monitoring to identify and correct errors.

### Addressing Limitations and Risks

A critical aspect of justifying this project involves acknowledging its limitations and the strategies employed to mitigate associated risks. The primary limitation concerns the reliability of automated allergen detection, which cannot achieve perfect accuracy given the variability in how recipes present ingredient information. The project addresses this limitation through multiple mechanisms: conservative allergen flagging that prioritises false positives over false negatives, clear user communication about the tool's limitations, prominent disclaimers advising users to verify allergen information independently, and documentation of the allergen detection methodology to enable informed user assessment of the tool's reliability.

Another limitation relates to the sustainability of web scraping approaches, given that website structures change over time and may break scraping implementations. The project addresses this through the implementation of fallback mechanisms, including mock data that ensures basic functionality even when live scraping fails, and modular design that facilitates updates to scraping logic when website structures change. Additionally, the project's focus on educational purposes and limited scale reduces the likelihood of causing harm to data sources, as the scraping activities remain within bounds that are unlikely to impact server performance or violate reasonable access expectations.

Rennie et al. (2020) emphasise the importance of ongoing ethical reflection and willingness to adjust practices as new challenges emerge. For this project, this means establishing mechanisms for monitoring system performance, collecting user feedback about accuracy and usability, and remaining open to modifying approaches when ethical concerns are identified. The authors argue that ethical web scraping is not a static achievement but an ongoing process of evaluation and adjustment. This perspective aligns with the iterative nature of software development and suggests that ethical considerations should be integrated into all phases of the project lifecycle, from initial design through deployment and maintenance.

### Contribution to Knowledge and Practice

This project contributes to both theoretical understanding and practical application in several ways. Theoretically, it demonstrates how ethical frameworks developed in academic literature can be operationalised in working systems, providing a concrete example that may inform future projects at the intersection of web scraping and digital health. The project bridges the gap between abstract ethical principles and practical implementation challenges, offering insights that may be valuable for other researchers and developers working in similar domains.

Practically, the project creates a functional tool that addresses a real need while modelling responsible data collection practices. By implementing the ethical principles articulated by Krotov et al. (2020) and Rennie et al. The system's design choices, from its conservative allergen detection approach to its transparent communication with users, reflect a commitment to balancing utility with ethical responsibility.

The project also contributes to the growing body of work on digital allergen management, an area that has received limited attention in the academic literature despite its public health significance. By documenting the development process, including the ethical decisions made and the technical challenges encountered, the project provides a resource for others seeking to develop similar applications. This documentation serves an educational function, making visible the ethical decision-making processes that often remain implicit in technical implementations. As Luscombe et al. ## Conclusion

This literature review has examined the ethical, legal, and technical dimensions of web scraping as they relate to the development of an allergen-filtered recipe search system. The review has established that contemporary scholarship provides clear guidance on ethical web scraping practices, centred on principles of transparency, respect for data sources, compliance with technical and legal standards, and careful consideration of potential harms. The application of these principles to allergen management systems introduces additional considerations related to data quality, user safety, and the heightened ethical obligations that accompany health-related applications.

The synthesis of this literature provides a robust foundation for the proposed project, demonstrating how ethical frameworks can be integrated into practical implementations that serve genuine needs while respecting the rights and interests of all stakeholders. The project's approach to rate limiting, transparent identification, and quality assurance reflects current best practices as articulated in recent peer-reviewed literature spanning multiple disciplines including information systems, public health, and archaeology. By situating the technical implementation within this comprehensive ethical framework, the project contributes to the ongoing development of responsible web scraping practices while addressing a meaningful public health need through the creation of a tool that assists individuals with food allergies in making safe dietary choices.

The interdisciplinary nature of this review, drawing on sources from Krotov et al. (2020), Rennie et al. (2020), and Luscombe et al. (2024), demonstrates the complexity of developing ethical web-based health applications. Each source contributes unique insights that collectively inform a comprehensive approach to allergen-filtered recipe search systems. The convergence of these diverse perspectives supports the conclusion that such systems can be developed responsibly when grounded in established ethical principles, informed by technical best practices, and motivated by genuine public health needs. The review has shown that ethical web scraping requires ongoing attention to legal frameworks, respect for data sources, commitment to data quality, and recognition of the special obligations that accompany health-related applications.
